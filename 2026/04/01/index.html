Macroeconomic Outlook

Growth & Labor

  • Stagflationary crosscurrents are solidifying. The unemployment rate has ticked up to 4.4% (from 4.1% last summer), while job openings have fallen to 6.88M.
  • Consumer sentiment remains deeply depressed at 56.6, reflecting exhaustion from sticky prices and geopolitical uncertainty.
  • Despite labor softening, industrial production remains resilient (102.5), keeping a floor under cyclical growth.

Inflation & Fixed Income

  • Core CPI and PCE continue a steady upward grind, complicating the Fed's easing path.
  • The yield curve has decisively un-inverted. The 10-year Treasury sits at 4.35% while the 2-year is at 3.82% (+53 bps spread), reflecting higher term premiums and entrenched inflation expectations.
  • Kevin Warsh is heavily favored to be confirmed as the next Fed Chair by May. Markets are pricing in a "Pause-Pause-Cut" trajectory for upcoming FOMC meetings, keeping the terminal rate higher for longer (around 3.5%–4.0%).
  • The U.S. Dollar remains exceptionally strong (Broad Index ~120.8), acting as a headwind for multinational earnings repatriations.

Geopolitics & Commodities

Energy & Middle East

  • WTI crude is experiencing violent volatility, spiking to nearly $99/bbl in late March before retracing to ~$89/bbl on the "Hormuz Hope" rally.
  • A U.S.-Iran ceasefire is highly anticipated by late April or May. If the Strait of Hormuz reopens, expect a sharp unwind in the geopolitical risk premium for oil. Conversely, a breakdown in talks easily puts $120+/bbl back in play.

Precious Metals

  • Gold is undergoing a massive structural re-rating. Markets are pricing in extreme tail-risk scenarios, with expectations of gold breaching $5,000/oz by summer. This reflects deep systemic hedging against U.S. debt monetization, NATO fragmentation, and Middle East instability.
